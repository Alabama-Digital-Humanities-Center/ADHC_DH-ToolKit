# Using the Custom Ontology Module in Omeka S
#### [Made by Sara Whitver with Scribe](https://scribehow.com/o/Q5Aaj_WQQLSLiZKvgOpJpw/viewer/Using_the_Custom_Ontology_Module_in_Omeka_S__lZlfmCnWRBWpUjR3xLo8xQ)
The Custom Ontology module for Omeka S, created by Daniel Berthereau, enables users to create specific classes and properties to describe resources when no standard ontologies can be used.

1\. From the dashboard of your Omeka S project, find the link to Custom Ontology in the modules section of the left hand menu.

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/c775b310-f0f5-4373-8a66-64082a6bf251/user_cropped_screenshot_058b50db50f44cd9a58c58cb81267ae6_text_export.jpeg)


Tip: The [Custom Ontology module](https://omeka.org/s/modules/CustomOntology/) is a tool that lets you develop your own set of hierarchical descriptive terms for your project. Think of the hierarchy like a tree. The broad terms are at the top and the narrow terms are at the bottom.


2\. An ontology is a way of showing the properties of a subject area and how they are related, by defining a set of terms and relational expressions that represent the entities in that subject area." (from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_\(information_science\))).

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/24b8b93d-525b-4a24-a3e3-6b56a1e074cf/screenshot_4bfa84c3a3c0407f89380c32adc40691_text_export.jpeg)


3\. Your first step in creating a custom ontology in Omeka S is naming the ontology. This includes creating a namespace uri (unique resource identifier), a prefix (a shortened version ofthe name) and a label (the full version of the name of the ontology).

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/a7b38cfa-96e1-4c70-83d6-00cbf226b809/screenshot_6d39a4b10c014f94a99bb390cb9e4c6c_text_export.jpeg)


Tip: Tip! Use a plain text editor like Notebook or Notepad to type your text out and then copy and paste it into the fields in the Custom Ontology module. This way you can keep track of the order and syntax of each element you are creating.

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/029f13fa-1242-4577-b065-228d0c0fce9b/screenshot_c11068ff7ee944bd9142d407705673b8_text_export.jpeg)


4\. Each element entered in the "create a specific ontology" fields must follow the specific syntax that was demonstrated in the prompt. If you do not follow the syntax, Omeka will send you an error message with information about how to fix your mistake.

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/a8969585-cae2-40bf-8369-93e65d12cbe1/screenshot_ce87b9c657a747dc9e894b692c6fdd25_text_export.jpeg)


5\. The next step in creating a custom ontology is creating the classes belonging to the ontology. Classes are broad names for groups of things that share characteristics.

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/e5231b9a-3557-4a71-87b7-d46538b50bbf/user_cropped_screenshot_099a1f0892434634ad480814f26c1581_text_export.jpeg)


Tip: You can return to your .txt document where you have prepared this information with the correct syntax. Copy and paste it into the form!

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/b4b8424c-aec7-428d-a28b-a4db0b69200a/user_cropped_screenshot_6a420fdac435462f92150f7f78249b26_text_export.jpeg)


6\. This is what your entries for classes should look. Pay special attention to the syntax of your entries, especially the spacing and the capitalization of words.

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/bf980154-33a6-43ec-b1ec-7734b08b6420/screenshot_5e98c76d54ae471bb29a0d282ece54f9_text_export.jpeg)


7\. The last section of your ontology defines the properties of your ontology. The names of the properties are the names of the fields that will be available to you when you are describing your objects in your Omeka S project.

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/8fe0ea21-1d41-4085-8f17-a3157521dd9a/screenshot_827c97a1104e4126a44c0d544f79c1cd_text_export.jpeg)


8\. Once again, you can prepare this text ahead of time in a .txt file so that when you get to this element on the Custom Ontology set up page, all you have to do is copy and paste. As always, it's important to pay attention to syntax here!

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/54ab30b2-e3e0-46c2-bfe5-134c70933b54/screenshot_888317b4994f4dd3a7ff0f33e7c35fd9_text_export.jpeg)


9\. When you have entered all of your information, scroll back to the top of the window and hit "Submit." If you have any errors, this is where Omeka will flag them and you can go back and fix them. When all of your errors are correct and your ontology has been successfully created, you will see this message.

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/37370cfe-0d3a-4bfa-ade4-7e11529338e8/user_cropped_screenshot_1d32055a12dc43128a7ca846f429d59d_text_export.jpeg)


Tip: How to double check that your new ontology is available to use? Just check in the Vocabularies section of your project. You should see your new group listed with the number of classes and properties you have created.

![](https://colony-recorder.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/files/2025-04-07/7f6a64f1-210e-4d1e-90c2-8f0629390a8b/user_cropped_screenshot_124ac462f8414393b0e6b2268fcd0eff_text_export.jpeg)
